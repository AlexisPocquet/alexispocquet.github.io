I am currently a student in the MPRI (Parisian Master of Research in Computer Science). My research interests include:

  • On the one hand, the semantics of programming languages and program verification through abstract interpretation, deductive methods, and proof assistants.
  • On the other hand—and more centrally—my interests lie in Homotopy Type Theory (HoTT), and more broadly in the higher-dimensional structures arising in dependent type theory and their connections with homotopy theory.

What draws me to both of these areas is the structural mode of reasoning they require. I particularly enjoy identifying and studying the abstract patterns that organize mathematical and computational objects, as well as using category theory as a language for expressing and relating these structures.

More recently, I have studied higher-dimensional naturality constructions for Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis globular weak ω-categories within CaTT, a type theory introduced by Finster and Mimram and subsequently investigated extensively by Benjamin. This work aims to make explicit and isolate a sophisticated transport structure associated with Martin-Löf types, which had previously been treated only implicitly in HoTT through the J eliminator.

Research Experience

  • M1 Research Internship Université Paris-Saclay, LMF · Thibaut Benjamin · march 2026 to july 2026

    For my last M1 semester, I worked under the supervision of Thibaut Benjamin, on augmenting the dependent type theory CaTT (a type theory for globular weak ω-categories) with parametricity. It turns out that this new theory exhibits some complex transport structure for Martin-Löf types, which had never been pointed out because of J eliminator expressiveness. This structure paves the way for studying some homotopy theory and the Homotopy Hypothesis.

  • L3 Research Work CNRS, ENS Paris, Antique team · Jérôme Féret and Jérôme Boillot · january 2025 to july 2025

    Over my last L3 semester, I worked with the Antique team (CNRS, ENS Ulm) on Abstract Interpretation and OS verification on IA-32. I studied the reduced product construction for abstract domains, then introduced an intermediate language with formal semantics and a sound over-approximation stable under task execution. [more] Abstract Interpretation, introduced by Radhia and Patrick Cousot in the late 1970s, formalizes program analysis as computations over over-approximations of reachable states — ensuring termination while retaining enough precision to infer meaningful invariants. The reduced product is a general construction for combining abstract domains; my semester work studied several instantiations, including those based on input/output channels and ghost quantities.

Programming

  • Language Pipelines 2024 — 2026

    Two compiler projects tracing a clear progression. KawaLang (Université Paris-Saclay, 2024) is a sequential interpreter for an OO toy language. MiniGoLang (École Polytechnique, 2026) reflects a more disciplined approach — monadic error accumulation across the full front-end, a typed-AST rewriting pass, and x86-64 code generation. [more] Importantly, the first pipeline fails eagerly on the first error and does not distinguish between the IO of the compiler and its analysis phases. The second one accumulates diagnostics via a dedicated Error module and separates concerns between the rewriting and emission phases. The compiler passes all provided test cases, including nested struct access, forward calls, and mutually recursive functions. See the implementation report for details.

  • TinyProver fall 2025

    An interactive proof assistant developed for the computational logic course at École Polytechnique, implementing two independent provers in OCaml: one for propositional logic via STLC, one for a minimal kernel of dependent type theory. [more] The STLC prover supports bidirectional type checking and an interactive tactic mode with sequent manipulation. The dependent types kernel implements the core of Martin-Löf type theory: substitution, parallel beta-reduction, alpha-conversion, and bidirectional inference for Pi-types, natural numbers, and propositional equality. The hardest part was normalization: a naive fixpoint fails on terms defined by recursion over Nat, requiring a more careful reduction strategy. The project ends with a reflection on the gap between a minimal kernel and a usable proof assistant.

  • MiniRust spring 2025

    A partial implementation of Rust's ownership discipline, developed for the advanced functional programming course at ENS Paris-Saclay. The pipeline runs a type checker and a borrow checker before emitting C. [more] The borrow checker handles the common cases but does not cover all edge cases — the interesting challenge was deciding where to draw the line and document the gaps honestly. On the backend, the most technically involved part is struct dependency resolution: a postfix DFS over a dependency graph ensures definitions are emitted in the right order, with cyclic dependencies through indirection handled implicitly. 91 of 94 tests pass.
