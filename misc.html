Below is a list of facts about the things I study. They are not meant to be understood as formal results, because they might just be rigorously wrong. Though I'm writing them informally, mainly because when I discovered them in the litterature or talking with others for the first time, I felt like a significant part of my little world had changed.

why should we laxify things ?

Laxifying a categorical construction allows one to discover more structure on it. The stricter our structures, the lower the dimensions, the less information. However, laxifying implies working with more complex objects, as strict equalities become cells. It is analogous to the fact that an intentional type theory allowing few reductions (/strict equalities) tends to make the proofs (/the structures of cells) more tedious.

indexed categories and Grothendieck fibrations

A family of sets $(X_j)_{j \in J}$ indexed by a set $J$ can be seen in two ways:

  • as a functor $A : J \to \mathbf{Set}$, which sends $j$ to $X_j$;
  • as a map $p : X \to J$, where $X = \coprod_{j} X_j$, so that $X_j = p^{-1}(j)$.

The maps $p$ are a very simple notion of fibration: each $X_j$ is the fibre above $j$. When $J$ becomes a category, a lifting condition appears on $p$. It is exactly what is needed for the whole thing to behave correctly, i.e. for the fibres to vary functorially along the arrows of $J$. The $p$'s obtained this way are the [discrete fibrations]. The relation between both points of view can be synthesized as: \[ [J \rightarrow \mathbf{Set}] \space \cong \space DFib(J) \] where $DFib(J)$ is the structure of discrete fibrations on $J$.

Now, generalize the correspondence between the two points of view: take $A$ to be of the form $J \to \mathbf{Cat}$ instead of $J \to \mathbf{Set}$, so that each $X_j$ is now a category. One can then arrange things so that the $p$'s become a generalization of discrete fibrations: these are the [Grothendieck fibrations], and the passage from $A$ to $p$ is the [Grothendieck construction].

categorical semantics as a lense on syntax

Syntactic formal systems are complex, rigid and raw objects to work with. Studying their semantics systematically needs one to use other complex tools as rewriting systems ($\beta$-reduction, local graph rewriting...), partially ordered sets (DCPO, domains...) but also categories ($\infty$-toposes, $\star$-autonomous categories...).

The latter have been used proficiently for several decades now. And I feel like categories allow one to formalize, dissect and fully understand the essence of a lot of syntactical intuitive but peculiar constructions. For instance, when I first learned functional programming, I understood that curryfication and nested evaluation were linked, but I did not know how and why. Understanding the closure property of cartesian closed categories as models of simply typed lambda-calculus allowed me to fully and precisely understand this strange link.

And by the way, thing is yet another cited example of a strange and complex property in type theory that can be simply described as a universal property. The first one being the characterization of TT with natural models.
